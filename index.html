About Chen Lab

Dedicated to advancing scientific knowledge through innovative research and collaboration

Chen Lab is committed to conducting high-quality research that pushes the boundaries of biological science. With state-of-the-art equipment and a talented team of researchers, we tackle challenging problems in molecular biology and genetics, with a focus on understanding how gene regulation evolves across vertebrate species.

We believe in the power of collaboration and open science. Our research findings are published in leading scientific journals and presented at international conferences, contributing to the global scientific community's understanding of evolutionary biology and gene regulation.

Research Focus Areas

We pursue research across multiple disciplines to understand biological systems and evolutionary processes

🧪

Molecular Biology

Investigating fundamental molecular processes including DNA replication, transcription, and protein synthesis. Our work contributes to understanding how genes are regulated and expressed in different tissues and developmental stages.

🔬

Genetic Engineering

Developing advanced genetic tools and techniques for precise DNA manipulation. We explore applications in agriculture, medicine, and biotechnology with a focus on understanding functional genomics.

🦠

Cell Biology

Studying cellular structures, functions, and interactions. Our research examines cellular signaling, metabolism, and disease mechanisms at the molecular level, with emphasis on cell-type specific gene regulation.

🧬

Genomics

Analyzing whole-genome sequences to understand genetic diversity and evolution. We use bioinformatic approaches to identify novel genetic factors and regulatory elements in disease.

💊

Biomedical Research

Developing therapeutic approaches for human disease. Our translational research bridges basic science with clinical applications, understanding how regulatory evolution affects disease susceptibility.

🌿

Evolutionary Biology

Designing studies to examine how gene regulatory networks evolve across species. We investigate evolutionary mechanisms underlying phenotypic diversity in vertebrate tissues.
